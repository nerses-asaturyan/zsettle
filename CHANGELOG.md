# Changelog

## 0.1.2 (28 October 2026)

- Wallet in the browser: Create a wallet and Restore now say what the wallet is doing while it starts (downloading, starting its threads, starting its worker) instead of showing nothing until it is up. A start step that takes more than a minute, or a worker that crashes as it starts, now ends with an error that names the step instead of waiting for ever.

## 0.1.1 (27 October 2026)

- Wallet in the browser: it starts on Safari 26.0 to 26.2 (iPhone, iPad and Mac). Those versions check some memory operations in one thread against the memory's size from before another thread grew it, so the wallet stopped with "Out of bounds memory access" as its threads started. On Safari and the other WebKit browsers the page now grows the wallet's memory once, by 512 MB (less if the browser refuses), before any other thread runs, and the threads take their memory from it. A purchase needs about 150 MB. Chrome, Edge and Firefox are unchanged.

- App: a proof's QR code is smaller and bigger on screen. It carries the proof's fields in capitals and digits, which QR codes pack tighter (65 squares wide instead of 81 to 85), and on a computer it is drawn up to 440 pixels wide. The camera asks for a sharper picture (1920 by 1080), the reader starts without waiting for an event Safari may not send and tries each frame at a few crops and scales (jsQR misses a slightly blurred code at some sizes), and the hint says to hold the phone about 20 cm away. The checker turns the compact code back into the usual proof text. The QR encoder gains alphanumeric mode; byte mode codes are unchanged.
- App: unlocking says "Opening your wallet…" once the passkey has answered.
- App: a network switch. The Testnet or Mainnet label in the top bar and the sidebar, and Network in the menu, move the browser wallet between the networks without a second unlock: the same 24 words and lock, with each network's own keys, coins, items, shops and history (the first switch starts that network at a recent block). On mainnet a notice under the top bar on every page says the coins are real and the software is not audited. A switch waits for a running sync or task, closes an open shop, and never replaces another wallet the browser holds for that network. The start page no longer offers a second wallet, with new words, for the other network.
- App: the wallet's "Catching up with the chain" line clears as soon as a sync ends; it stayed up to two seconds longer.
- Wallet in the browser: creating items and cancelling take coins from their first confirmation too, like Send and Buy (they still waited 10 blocks for coins from another wallet, while the page said they could be spent). Payments, new items and cancels anchor three blocks deep whenever notes that deep can pay, as ZIP 315 wallets do; only coins from the newest blocks anchor at the tip. A purchase takes a note that pays the price and the fee exactly, or with change above dust, not just the oldest note.
- House shop: an old item moves only in a round where a coin can pay the move's fee, and the faucet tries to pay only when the house's coins can, so a house without spendable coins no longer stops and restarts its shop every round (or every 30 seconds while a faucet payment waits); buyers no longer meet a closed shop meanwhile.
- Buying: the price the buyer confirmed goes with the purchase. A shop that changed its price after the buyer opened it is not paid the new price unseen: the purchase stops before any coins move and says so.
- App: the camera stops when the scan sheet is closed before the camera starts, or when the QR reader fails to load.
- App: item pictures fill their tiles in Safari too (Safari sized them from the emoji, as a small box in the corner).
- App: a purchase that no single note can pay says so; it said the coins were still confirming.
- App: a browser wallet that stopped on an error says so and offers a reload; it showed an empty wallet with 0 ZEC instead. The screenshots show the current app.
- CLI: `zsettle pay` reads `ZSETTLE_WALLET`, `ZSETTLE_BUYER_ACCOUNT` and `ZSETTLE_IDENTITY` like the other wallet commands, so it runs with `env.sh` sourced, as the docs say.
- App: a proof's QR code can be scanned in every browser with a camera: the browser's barcode detector where it has one (Chrome, Edge, Samsung Internet on Android), else jsQR 1.4.0 (Apache-2.0, served as `/jsqr.js` and loaded on first use), so on an iPhone too.
- Wallet in the browser, after a phone test: the unlock screen shows while the engine loads; the wallet syncs while the page is in front, and the sync chip and the buy steps show the blocks left; a purchase shows five steps; Send and Buy spend coins from their first confirmation (a trade is one transaction: a reorganisation that undid the paying coins would undo the trade too); passkeys are discoverable (Android keeps only those in Google Password Manager, which derives the key) and the unlock asks for the passkey straight from the tap (Safari); a page update held back by an open sheet happens when it closes.
- House shop: an item listed for over 2,000 blocks moves to a fresh note, one per round, so a new buyer's first scan of the shop's item key stays short; a moved item keeps its name; the shop opens before old items move after a restart.
- App: a button inside a field (the shop address's Open) no longer covers the field's edge.

- One app: on a hosted server the app at `/` is the wallet, and it runs in the browser. The separate `/wallet/` page is gone (`/wallet` and `/wallet/...` answer `301` to `/`). The page loads the wallet engine (`/engine.js`, `/worker.js`, `/pkg/`); the 24 words and the wallet database stay in the browser's IndexedDB, encrypted under a passkey (WebAuthn PRF) or a passphrase (PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds, then AES-GCM), one wallet per network. Proofs and signatures are made in the page, on the testnet or mainnet.
- Selling from the browser: minting, listing, delisting and cancelling run in the page, and a shop is open while its page is open. The page holds the item key in the wallet's worker, checks each offer with the seller service's checks and signs its half. The server keeps a mailbox per browser shop (`/api/mailbox/<code>/...`, every request signed with the item key) and buyers use the usual `/shop/<code>/...` requests.
- Item histories and ownership-proof checks run in the browser too, with the same tracer as `zsettle verify` and `zsettle check-proof`, fed with blocks through the relay.
- The hosted server keeps no visitor wallets. Removed: wallet create, unlock and sign-in, wallet codes, the session cookie, passkey sign-in with the site's PRF salt (`prf.salt` is no longer used), a shop process per visitor, the idle lock, the removal of unused wallets, and `--allow-hosted-mainnet`. `--web-wallet` is now required in hosted mode, and `--network` only sets the network of the house shop and its faucet. The server serves the page and the engine, the chain relay, `/api/hello`, `/api/shops`, `/api/wallet-faucet`, the shop mailboxes, `/shop/<code>/...`, and its own house shop; any other `/api/` call answers `404`. Wallet folders left by the old hosted mode stay on disk but are not loaded. Local mode is unchanged.
- A wallet that runs in the browser (`crates/zsettle-web`, served by `zsettle-app --web-wallet`): the core compiled to WebAssembly, with proving on a thread pool. The recovery phrase is made in the page and kept there, encrypted under a passkey or a passphrase, with the wallet database; the server only relays public chain data (`/api/chain/`) and passes offers to its shops. It creates and restores wallets on the testnet or mainnet, syncs, receives, sends, buys from the server's shops and makes ownership proofs. `zsettle-core` gains a `native` feature for the network, file and terminal parts; without it the core builds for the browser. The Ironwood proving key is built once per process.
- Released crates only: the NU7 release line (`zcash_client_backend` 0.25, `zcash_client_sqlite` 0.23, `zcash_primitives` 0.31, `zcash_protocol` 0.11, `pczt` 0.10, `orchard` 0.16, published 21 October 2026, which set the mainnet NU7 activation at block 3,543,405) replaces both the NU6.3 line on `main` and the pre-releases on `nu7-testnet`. One build serves mainnet and the testnet and crosses the mainnet activation by itself. Patch updates include rustls 0.23.46, rustls-webpki 0.103.16 and webpki-roots 1.0.10, which fix published security advisories.
- A trade's expiry follows NU7's 25-second blocks: the wallet library's standard expiry is 40 blocks before NU7 and 120 from NU7 (ZIP 218), and trades, self-transfers and the note query now use exactly that, so a trade still carries no distinguishing expiry on an NU7 chain. Listing expiry limits are read as 75-second reference blocks (default 40, about 50 minutes), and the ownership-proof age defaults to about 50 minutes.
- Ownership proofs are non-interactive (format version 2): `prove` signs the note, the item key and the last scanned block's height and hash, so a proof is made once and shared like an address, and `check-proof` accepts it while that block is on its chain and recent (`--max-age`, default about 50 minutes). An optional signed `--label` names the holder; `--challenge` is now optional and turns a proof into a live check. The app's proof sheets follow: share by default, live check optional. Version 1 proofs are refused with a request for a new one.
### Before the one app

These entries describe the builds before the one app. The hosted server's visitor wallets, sign-in, wallet codes, session cookies and per-visitor shops they mention were removed by it (see above).

- Docs, from the same red team: the "transparent addresses are never sent" and on-chain-indistinguishability claims now state what the code actually enforces (and that a non-default confirmation count is a fingerprint); the ownership-proof pages add the relay limitation, the height it reveals, and that ZEC stays private only with a separate item account; the security page documents why reusing the item key to sign messages is safe and that delisting does not cancel signed offers; the protocol spec notes the item pairing is enforced by the buyer, not the seller.
- Security hardening from an independent crypto red team: `publish` refuses an expiry limit above the default (no long free option for a buyer); the paying note is reserved before its offer leaves the wallet; the manual `sign` applies the same action-count cap as the service; `check-proof --private` hides the checked item from the lightwalletd server; the signed listings and ownership-proof structures reject unknown fields.
- App: a darker, blurred backdrop behind sheets, so a sheet no longer looks like it overlaps the balance card on a phone; an ownership proof can be shared straight to a messaging app.
- New items show as arriving right after they are created, before their block, like a bought item does.
- Hosted server: API calls are counted per signed-in wallet (per visitor only before sign-in), so people behind one shared address do not block each other; a wallet is removed only after a fresh look at the chain finds nothing; a faucet payment cut short by a restart is judged only once the wallet is an hour old; the `passkey:` passphrase shape needs a real credential id; a failed offer says whether the coins were freed or stay set aside.
- A note of an unmined transaction is counted only while that transaction can still be mined; the change of an expired or rejected payment no longer shows in the balance.
- The wallet records the transactions it builds itself (a trade, a rotation, a cancel) as soon as they are sent: the balance is right at once, change shows as settling, and a payment made meanwhile cannot pick a note that is already spent.
- App: a passkey wallet confirms every transaction with the passkey ("Confirm with passkey"); the balance card shows coins leaving after a purchase; a seller with an open shop sees confirmations (`zsettle height`); an ownership proof is shown as a QR code and the checker can scan it.

- Faucet: the payment's transaction is written before it is broadcast, so a paying process that is lost resends it instead of paying twice; payments cut short by a restart are looked at when the server starts, and a wallet that received nothing gets its chance back.
